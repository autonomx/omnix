### Ranking and classification outcome

The frozen **risk-adjusted** order `AENT > FEIM > TNON > ACVA > LBGJ` held up well. It should not be compared mechanically with end-of-day percentage gains because the ranking intentionally includes catalyst quality, price-band eligibility, execution quality, supply risk, and expected post-entry asymmetry. ACVA is the clearest example: it posted an enormous one-day percentage gain because the acquisition repriced the stock, yet once trading near $10.44 against a $10.50 cash offer it offered very little additional upside for the Omnix continuation strategy. FEIM, conversely, confirmed that a high-quality catalyst can support both gap retention and continued intraday range expansion even when it remains ineligible under the current price band.

### What was learned

**Observation 1 — catalyst quality plus execution eligibility must remain separate axes.** FEIM was arguably the strongest fundamental setup and delivered a confirmed +32.82% session, but it was correctly excluded from legacy AUTO PAPER because its price was far above the strategy band. This is useful research evidence for a future high-price variant, not permission to alter the live rule retrospectively.

**Observation 2 — merger gaps should remain a separate regime.** ACVA validated the distinction between **gap retention** and **tradable continuation**. A cash acquisition can produce one of the largest percentage gaps in the cohort while simultaneously producing one of the weakest incremental reward/risk profiles after the open.

**Observation 3 — large low-float moves do not falsify supply-risk warnings.** TNON's strong continuation coexisted with exactly the type of capital-structure risk flagged premarket. The later warrant-inducement transaction strengthens the methodology lesson that supply risk is an execution/variance dimension, not a directional prediction.

**Observation 4 — AENT supports ranking in-band executable opportunity separately from absolute catalyst strength.** Its earnings repricing and large volume expansion supported the #1 Omnix-style rank even though FEIM had the stronger fundamental catalyst. AENT fit the strategy's price universe and had a materially improved liquidity regime compared with its historical baseline.

**Observation 5 — missing catalyst remains a valid rejection gate.** LBGJ's regular-session weakness supports continuing to treat extreme low float as variance potential rather than a bullish edge by itself.

### Research hypotheses updated — observations only, not strategy changes

1. **Merger-arbitrage regime flag:** continue collecting samples where a cash-deal price caps upside. Hypothesis: these should be excluded from continuation ranking or scored in a dedicated gap-retention regime even when they dominate Finviz gainers.
2. **High-price catalyst cohort:** continue shadow-tracking >$20 earnings gaps such as FEIM separately. Hypothesis: catalyst persistence may remain strong, but spread behavior, dollar risk, and sizing require a separately validated parameter set before any eligibility change.
3. **Catalyst freshness decay:** TNON remains evidence that second-day momentum can persist, but the prior-day catalyst plus financing/supply interaction increases variance. Test freshness as a continuous prior rather than a binary “has catalyst” flag.
4. **Liquidity regime shift:** AENT supports measuring same-day volume expansion relative to historical average as an execution-quality update, while keeping extension risk independent.
5. **Signal evidence discipline:** store or retain authoritative causal one-minute bars whenever possible so future post-close reviews can classify V2 trigger success/failure rather than leaving the deterministic outcome unknown.
