### Research hypotheses — observations, not strategy changes

1. **Squeeze amplification deserves its own prospective score.** CHPT suggests that a credible fundamental catalyst plus unusually high short interest can produce realized expansion far beyond what catalyst quality alone predicts. This is a research hypothesis, not a rule change.
2. **Forward-guide quality does not immunize against opening overextension.** TLYS had one of the strongest fundamental reports in the cohort yet displayed a severe high-to-close-level fade in the provisional daily row. The hypothesis is that smaller vehicles with ~30% premarket extension need a larger extension penalty even when the catalyst is excellent.

4. **Low-float variance remains more predictable than low-float direction.** AEHL again supports the journal's existing view: tiny supply predicts range/instability better than it predicts a durable directional path.
