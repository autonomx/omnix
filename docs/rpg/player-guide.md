# Playing the RPG

Omnix's RPG is a story you play one turn at a time. You say what your hero does, in your own words; the game works out
what happens by its rules, and the storyteller tells it. The world keeps its own time: its people keep their days,
its troubles grow or ease, and coming back after a while finds it moved on.

## Starting

Open **RPG** and choose **Campaign Menu**:

- **Continue** picks up your last campaign.
- **New Campaign** starts one. Choose a world (the bundled Aurelia, Vesper-9 or The Market Road, or one from your
  library), an opening, and your hero: a name, pronouns and how they fight. With **World Forge** on, describe the
  campaign you want instead: Omnix makes a new world for it first and then sets out in it.
- **Save points** returns to a moment you saved (see *Undo and save points*).
- **Demo Session** opens a ready-made hero (level 14, at Glimmerdeep Pass) to try things out.
- **Load Game** lists your campaigns to open, rename or delete.

A campaign can be started in **hardcore** mode: losing a fight ends the hero's story, and there is no undo.

## A turn

Write what your hero does in **Your turn** and press Enter: "look around", "ask the innkeeper about the road north",
"buy two rations", "go to Skybridge Pass", "attack the Echo Wolf", "rest until morning". The suggestions under the
box are things that make sense now; choose one or write your own. When the game is not sure what you meant, it asks,
with choices.

The story shows what happened, once. Under it, the outcome card lists what changed: coin, items, health,
conditions, quest steps, offers and any dice that were rolled. When a roll decides something (persuading a guard,
climbing a wall, picking a lock), you see the roll, your skill and the difficulty. Failing changes the situation
rather than stopping the story.

Time passes with what you do: looking around takes a few minutes, waiting half an hour, a night's sleep until
morning. The world rail shows the day, the time, the weather and where you are.

A journey takes its road's time. A place further than one road away is set out for: you take the first road of the
way there ("You set out for Moonroot Ruins: the way goes by Skybridge Pass") and arrive somewhere on the way, with
whatever is there; say it again to go on.

## People

Talk to whoever is there. Each answers in their own voice about what they would know. They share some things
freely, some for a price, some only once they trust you, and their secrets never. Ask for work and someone who has
it offers you a quest. Ask a shopkeeper what they sell and they tell you, with prices.

People keep their days: a stallholder is at her stall by day and at the tavern in the evening. Someone you are with
does not vanish mid-conversation; you see them leave when their day turns, and others arrive.

## Trade

Coin is gold, silver and copper (1 gold is 100 copper; 1 silver is 10). Buy from the people who sell ("buy a hot
meal", "I'll take the lantern"). Sell what you carry to someone who buys that kind of thing; they pay part of its
price. A shop that sells out restocks over days, and when the roads are dangerous, goods from elsewhere cost more.

## Your hero and what you carry

The player rail shows health, stamina, mana, experience, coin and survival (hunger, thirst, fatigue). Eat, drink and
rest to keep them down; **Eat**, **Drink** and **Rest** put the turn in the box for you to send.

Open an item in **Inventory** to read it and use, equip, unequip, drop or sell it. A potion heals and closes a wound,
food eases hunger, a drink eases thirst. Something put down stays where you left it. You can give what you carry to
someone who is there ("give my dagger to Liora"): it is a gift, not a sale, and they think a little better of you. A night's sleep, in a room paid
for or at a camp in the wild, restores you; a short rest takes the edge off.

Experience comes from fights won, quests finished, checks passed and discoveries. Each level brings a choice: a
better attribute, a new ability, or more skill. Skills also improve as you use them.

## Fights

Attack a creature the world knows, or someone present, and a fight begins on the place's local map. Everyone rolls
for initiative; the fight panel shows the turn order and whose turn it is. On your turn, attack, move on the grid or
try to break away. Creatures fight in their own ways: wolves flee when badly hurt, bandits call for help, a guardian
never leaves its post. Some fights can be talked down or slipped past.

Creatures keep to the wild, where no one lives. Winning earns experience and whatever they drop. Losing is a
setback, not the end (except in hardcore): you wake later where you last rested, hurt and lighter in the purse.
Fleeing usually works, at a cost.

## Quests

The journal shows each quest's current step, sometimes with two ways forward. Quests can fail: a deadline passes,
someone dies, or you side against the giver. The world's troubles make their own quests ("the roads are dangerous:
clear the wolves from Skybridge Pass"), and dealing with them eases the trouble.

## Undo and save points

**Undo** (beside the submit button) takes back your last turn and restores the game as it was before it. Make a
named save point at any time from **Campaign Menu → Save points**, and return to it later. Neither is available in
hardcore campaigns.

## Coming back

Leave a campaign for ten minutes or more (close it, or switch away from the tab) and the world moves on while you
are gone. When you return, the story says what changed.

## If something goes wrong

- **"The storyteller is unavailable"**: the model did not answer in time. The turn still happened by the rules and
  people still answer with what is true; try again later for fuller narration.
- **"Your turn failed"**: nothing was taken. Send it again; a turn is never applied twice.
- **"Too many turns"**: you hit the hourly turn limit; the message says when you can play again.
- A map or grid view that is out of date is refused and redrawn as the campaign now stands.
